01 · Industry challenges

SIX POINTS WHERE LARGE PROJECTS LOSE MONEY

These are not one company's problems: they run through general contracting as a whole, and they all share a single root — data about reality arrives late, incomplete or in a disputable form.

01

Disputed progress levels

Monthly valuation rests on estimates and site walks. Any discrepancy between client, main contractor and subcontractor means weeks of negotiation and withheld payment.

02

Deviations spotted too late

A misplaced penetration or structural element surfaces only once it has been covered up. Fixing it costs orders of magnitude more than the survey that would have prevented it.

03

Earthworks and material quantities

Excavated and placed volumes are billed from delivery notes and cross sections. The actual volume of stockpiles is rarely verified against independent data.

04

High-risk manual inspection

Inspecting roofs, façades and tall structures requires scaffolding, rope access or a lift — safety risk, permitting and downtime all at once.

05

Proving subcontractor performance

In a dispute it is photographs against memory. There is no time-stamped, geometrically reliable record of the state of works to fall back on.

06

As-built documentation

The as-built dataset requested at handover is often reconstructed afterwards from memory. The model handed to the operator therefore does not describe what was actually built.

PROGRESS AND VALUATION

DEVIATION ANALYSIS

The difference between the built structure and the design model appears on a colour-coded map. Deviations can be read in millimetres, with critical points listed.

Scan-to-BIM registration in a shared coordinate system
Automatic flagging of out-of-tolerance elements
Checking penetrations, protrusions and out-of-plane deviations
The error surfaces before covering up, while it can still be fixed

PHASE 01

TENDER AND PREPARATION

A bid carries exactly as much risk as there is uncertainty about the initial state.

Survey of the existing state — laser scanning and drone photogrammetry capturing the exact geometry of the site and existing structures before the price is fixed.
Initial volume calculation — earthwork quantities computed from the terrain model for the cost estimate.
Recording the zero state — the condition of the site at handover documented, for later damage claims and boundary disputes.
PHASE 02

CONSTRUCTION

This is where the project's outcome is decided. Construction monitoring in repeating cycles.

Cyclical construction monitoring — monthly or fortnightly surveys, the same methodology every cycle, producing comparable data.
Design vs. reality deviation — scan-to-BIM comparison on a colour-coded deviation map, before anything is covered up.
Progress and valuation — progress certification based on measured data instead of estimates.
Inspection of high-risk zones — checking tall structures and façades without scaffolding or rope access.
PHASE 03

HANDOVER

Documentation is not an afterthought; it is a by-product of the process.

As-built model — from data collected during construction, not from memory.
Visual snag list — open items located on the model and the orthophoto.
Complete data handover — point cloud, orthophoto, model and reports in the format the client requires.
PHASE 04

OPERATION

Once the project closes, the data can still be maintained as a service.

Condition monitoring — repeated surveys to track wear and movement.
Warranty evidence — reliable data on the built state, usable in warranty disputes.
Base data for conversion — designing a later extension or refurbishment starts from a ready geometric basis.
